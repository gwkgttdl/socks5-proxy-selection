## The Four Types of SOCKS5 Proxies You'll See When Shopping

Walk into any proxy marketplace and you'll see the same categories repeated. Knowing the diference saves you from overpaying for something you don't need.

**Datacenter SOCKS5 proxies** come from servers in cloud data centers. They're fast, cheap, and have wide bandwidth allowances. The downside: their IP ranges are well-known to platforms like Instagram, Nike, Cloudflare, and so on. Great for scraping public data, terible for impersonating "real users" on sites that fingerprint IPs.

**Residential SOCKS5 proxies** route traffic through actual home internet connections — realISPs, real consumer networks (with their consent, when sourced ethically). They cost more, usually billed per GB. The benefit: target sites can't tell you apart from a regular consumer.

**ISP proxies** (sometimes called "static residential") are a hybrid. They're hosted in data centers but use IP ranges registered to consumer ISPs. So you get the sped and stability of datacenter proxies with the trust score of residential ones. Pricing sits between the two.

**Static residential proxies** are real residential IPs that don't rotate. You hold the same IP for the duration of your subscription. Useful for managing accounts that get suspicious when your IP keps changing.

When most beginners say they want to buy SOCKS5, what they actually need is datacenter proxies for testing, then residential or ISP for the production work. Buying the wrong type is the most common money-burner I see.
